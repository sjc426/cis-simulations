01 — about this course

WHAT YOU WILL LEARN

CIS 256 is a hands-on networking course built around the CCNA 200-301 blueprint. You will design, configure, and troubleshoot real enterprise networks using Cisco IOS — the same command-line interface used by network engineers worldwide. By the end of the semester you will have configured routers, built VLAN environments, deployed OSPF routing, secured networks with ACLs, and evaluated AI-driven network management tools.

🔌 Layer 2 — Weeks 1–4

VLANs, trunking, Spanning Tree Protocol, and inter-VLAN routing. The foundation everything else sits on.

🌐 Layer 3 — Weeks 5–11

Static routes, OSPFv2, OSPFv3, IPv6 addressing, and HSRP/VRRP redundancy protocols.

📡 Wireless & Edge — Wks 7, 10

802.11 band selection, channel planning, IoT devices, VoIP, and cloud-managed networking.

🛡️ Network Security — Wks 12–13

ACLs, NAT, DNS, DHCP, port security, BPDU guard, and firewall fundamentals.

🤖 AI & Automation — Wks 10, 14

Cisco AgenticOps, DevNet sandboxes, and evaluating AI-generated network configurations critically.

🏗️ Capstone — Week 14

Design and document a full enterprise network for Keystone Community Health. Real brief, real deliverable.

federal laws you must know

THE LAWS THAT APPLY TO YOU

These are not hypothetical. Network engineering students have been prosecuted under every one of these statutes. Know them before you touch a tool.

Computer Fraud and Abuse Act (CFAA) — 18 U.S.C. § 1030

Criminalizes unauthorized access to computers and networks. Penalties range from 1 to 20 years in federal prison depending on intent and damage. "I was just curious" is not a defense. "I didn't know it was unauthorized" is not a defense.

Wiretap Act — 18 U.S.C. § 2511

Prohibits intercepting electronic communications without authorization. Running Wireshark on a network you do not own — even passively, even without reading the packets — is a federal violation. The capture itself is the crime.

Electronic Communications Privacy Act (ECPA)

Extends wiretap protections to stored electronic data. Accessing someone's stored emails, files, or network logs without authorization violates federal law even if no data is modified or taken.

Pennsylvania Computer Hacking Laws — 18 Pa. C.S. § 7611–7615

State-level offenses that mirror federal law. Pennsylvania prosecutes independently of federal charges. You can face both simultaneously — in separate courts, with separate sentences.

real cases — real consequences

THIS HAS HAPPENED TO STUDENTS LIKE YOU

Case 1 — The Curious Student

A community college student ran nmap against the college network "just to see what was open." The IT department flagged the scan within minutes. The student faced suspension and a referral to campus security. Criminal charges were only avoided because the institution chose not to press them — that decision was not the student's to make.

Case 2 — The Wi-Fi Sniffer

A student ran Wireshark at a coffee shop — passively capturing traffic, not reading it. Federal investigators do not distinguish between passive and active capture. The Wiretap Act criminalizes the interception itself. The student was charged under 18 U.S.C. § 2511.

Case 3 — The "Helpful Researcher"

A student scanned a local business network to identify vulnerabilities and planned to report them anonymously. Written authorization did not exist. Intent is irrelevant under the CFAA. The student was prosecuted for unauthorized access. The business pressed charges. The student did not finish their degree.
